# Research tactics log

This file records which high-level problem-solving tactics were actually used, what they produced, and which attractive techniques were screened out. The goal is to make mathematical search replayable rather than retrospectively mythologized.

## T0 — Morph rule: change representation before strengthening the theorem

**Applied.**

Original object: complete ABA types and specification predicates.

Representation sequence:

1. complete type -> zero/nonzero support on Boolean minterm cells;
2. coordinate restriction -> projection of supports;
3. two-block projection fiber -> bipartite graph with no isolated vertices;
4. multi-block projection fiber -> multipartite hypergraph with surjective coordinate projections;
5. atomic equation -> intersection query on the support;
6. family of atomic equations -> OR-code / join-semilattice homomorphism;
7. predicate selection -> generalized group testing / separable code problem.

**Payoff:** exact type counts, exact extension fibers, exact atomic-test lower bounds, and an NP-hardness transfer all became transparent after the representation changes.

## T1 — Tao: try simpler cases first

Source: Terence Tao, `245A: Problem solving strategies`.

**Applied.** Work through support-row counts `r=1,2,3,4` before generalizing.

Observed:

- `r=1`: 1 candidate;
- `r=2`: 7 candidates;
- `r=3`: 25 candidates;
- `r=4`: 79 candidates.

The pattern `3^r-2` exposed the row-wise three-choice mechanism `{left,right,both}` with two globally forbidden constant choices.

## T2 — Tao: actively try to build a counterexample

Source: Tao's problem-solving list recommends trying to construct a picture satisfying the hypotheses but violating the conclusion; the way the attempt fails can reveal the proof mechanism.

**Applied.** The initial information bound suggested seven predicate bits might identify the 79-state hardest fiber. Instead of trying to construct a clever 7-test atomic code, test the nearly identical candidates

`U` and `U\{v}`.

The attempt fails maximally: the only intersection query distinguishing them is `{v}`. Repeating for every `v` forces all eight singleton queries.

**Payoff:** killed the false atomic 7-bit conjecture and produced the general co-atom obstruction theorem.

## T3 — Tao: abstraction followed by a different special case

Source: Tao describes the advanced move of abstracting away irrelevant structure and then studying a simpler special case of the abstract problem.

**Applied.** Abstract ABA atomic predicates to generalized group testing on an arbitrary candidate set-system `E`. Then specialize back not to arbitrary ABA types but to the structured family `F_r` of row/column-surjective support matrices.

**Payoff:** imported known complexity results without confusing them with new Tau mathematics, while the special ABA family admitted a much stronger exact theorem than the general literature bound.

## T4 — Tao: exploit symmetry / work by orbits

Source: Tao's symmetry-reduction tactic.

**Applied.** The `(m,x)`/`y` fiber is invariant under row permutations and column swap. Fiber cardinality depends only on the support sizes `(r,c)`, not on named cells.

This reduced 45 ocLTL projection pairs to a handful of orbit classes and led to the formula for `r x c` 0/1 matrices with no zero row or column.

The multi-block version depends only on `(r_1,...,r_b)`.

## T5 — Tao: use generators and closure

Source: Tao's tactic of proving a property on generators and showing closure under the operations generating the class.

**Applied.** Every Boolean term is generated by the minterm cells under finite joins. Atomic equations therefore reduce to behavior on the minterm generators. The support evaluation law

`f(S)!=0 iff S intersects A_f`

then handles all Boolean terms at once.

## T6 — information/entropy heuristic, then structural correction

**Applied, but only as a heuristic.**

The 79-state fiber gives the Shannon/counting lower bound

`ceil(log2 79)=7`.

That lower bound is achievable by arbitrary definable predicates but not by atomic equations. The gap revealed a hidden algebraic constraint: atomic signatures are coordinatewise ORs of cell codewords.

**Lesson:** entropy says how many unrestricted bits are necessary; it does not characterize what a syntactically constrained observation language can encode.

## T7 — Gowers/Fields-medalist technique: recast set problems algebraically

Source: Timothy Gowers, `Dimension arguments in combinatorics`, where characteristic vectors and algebraic structure reveal extremal bounds.

**Applied in screened form.** We recast support families as Boolean vectors and atomic tests as columns in a Boolean OR-semimodule / join-semilattice rather than immediately forcing ordinary linear algebra over a field.

Why ordinary dimension was not used for the current lower bound: the co-atom pairs force unique singleton queries directly, giving an exact result stronger and simpler than a rank bound. Gowers's own heuristic says linear algebra is especially suggestive when many extremizers exist; here the critical queries are individually forced.

**Future use:** rank/dimension methods may become relevant for restricted formula families where singleton forcing disappears but many optimal encodings exist.

## T8 — Noether criterion: identify the inner ground

For each derived formula, record the structural mechanism:

- `|T_k|=2^(2^k)-1`: nonempty support on `2^k` Venn cells;
- extension count `(2^(2^r)-1)^s`: independent nonempty refinement of every nonzero coarse cell;
- fiber count: spanning multipartite hypergraphs / no isolated vertices;
- atomic-test lower bound: full support plus all co-atoms force private coordinates;
- fixed-point termination: finite orbit/type lattice, not a syntactic iteration miracle.

A proof that only certifies the count without exposing one of these mechanisms is considered incomplete for exposition even if formally correct.

## T9 — finite exhaustive search as falsifier, not universal proof

**Applied.** Small cases are enumerated exactly in Python. Optimization experiments were used to detect the atomic 8-test optimum before the human proof was found.

Promotion rule: once a finite search suggests a universal theorem, replace it with a structural proof or clearly limit the theorem to the exhausted finite domain.

## T10 — Gowers technique screened out: dimension argument

**Status:** not currently used.

The current exact lower bound is local: each co-atom pair forces one private query. A dimension proof would obscure rather than explain this mechanism. Reconsider dimension/rank only for candidate families lacking the full-plus-co-atoms obstruction.

## T11 — Tao tensor/amplification technique screened out

**Status:** not currently used.

The projection-fiber formulas already compose by Cartesian products and inclusion-exclusion. No inequality with a removable constant or multiplicative amplification gap has appeared. Do not use tensor-power language merely because the supports are products.

## T12 — next systematic tactic sequence

For each new OrbitSynthesis conjecture:

1. **Represent:** move from formulas to types/supports/orbits/hypergraphs/codes as appropriate.
2. **Tiny cases:** exhaust the smallest nontrivial arities.
3. **Attack:** search for minimal counterexamples and near-extreme pairs.
4. **Symmetry:** quotient the search by automorphisms before brute force.
5. **Invariant:** identify the statistic preserved by the operators.
6. **Adjacent theorem:** search for a structurally identical problem solved in another area; import only proved machinery.
7. **Explain:** produce an inner-ground proof, not only inequalities or computation.
8. **Formalize:** Lean-check claims whose dependency graph is available; otherwise label the exact missing formal dependency.
9. **Differential test:** compare executable semantics with Tau on bounded exhaustive suites.
10. **Promote or kill:** update the theorem/conjecture and negative-knowledge ledgers.
