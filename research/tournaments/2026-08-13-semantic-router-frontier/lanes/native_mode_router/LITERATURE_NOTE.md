# Literature note

The three-state structure used here is the classical flip-flop monoid: an identity together with two reset elements. A primary historical reference is Kenneth Krohn and John Rhodes, “Algebraic theory of machines. I. Prime decomposition theorem for finite semigroups and machines,” *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* 116 (1965), 450--464, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-1965-0188316-1`.

The paper should claim only the exact embedding into the present discriminator algebra and the resulting quantitative router refinement. It should not claim invention of the flip-flop monoid or of balanced associative evaluation.
