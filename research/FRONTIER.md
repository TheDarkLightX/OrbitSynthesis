## 1. Starting point: ocLTL

Ohad Asor's 2026 `ocLTL` paper studies LTL+Past over an effectively presented countable omega-categorical structure M with bounded cross-time data access. By Ryll-Nardzewski/Engeler/Svenonius, for each fixed k there are finitely many complete k-types, and two tuples have the same complete type exactly when they lie in the same Aut(M)-orbit.

The paper reduces realizability/synthesis to propositional LTL by replacing data formulas with complete types. For the three-variable presentation, the naive translation uses `|T_2| + |T_3|` propositional variables. Its own conclusion identifies **type enumeration as the main M-dependent blowup** and points to fixed-point-definable temporal fragments as especially promising because they can be decided directly over M without the full propositional encoding.

Source: Ohad Asor, *ocLTL: LTL Realizability and Synthesis Modulo omega-Categorical Structures*, arXiv:2605.12539 (2026), especially §§2,4,5.2,6,7.

## 2. What §5.2 actually gives us

Let Delta be the finite set of data subformulas occurring in a specification. Asor observes that the translated system does not always need the full `T_3` type. It needs the truth values of predicates in Delta together with enough restriction/shift information to maintain compatibility across steps. In the worst case Delta generates the full Boolean algebra of definable 3-ary sets, so worst-case avoidance of `T_3` is impossible; away from the worst case the generated algebra can be much coarser.

This gives a concrete mathematical object to study:

`B_Delta = the Boolean subalgebra of Def_k(M) generated by Delta.`

The first question is not "can we invent a minimizer?" but:

> What is the exact structure, size, and efficiently computable representation of `B_Delta`, and of the extra restriction/shift information required by synthesis?

## 3. Direct fixed points are a first-class frontier

For an omega-categorical M, every parameter-free definable k-ary relation is a finite union of complete k-types. Consequently the Boolean algebra `Def_k(M)` is finite. A monotone first-order operator on k-ary relations therefore induces a monotone map on a finite lattice, so its least and greatest fixed points stabilize after finitely many strict changes.

For a theory with an effective decision procedure / quantifier elimination, this suggests an algorithm that does **not explicitly enumerate all types**:

1. represent the current approximation by a first-order formula `psi_n`;
2. substitute `psi_n` into the positive relation variable of the fixed-point operator;
3. eliminate quantifiers / normalize;
4. decide whether `psi_{n+1} <-> psi_n` is valid in the theory;
5. stop at equality.

This is exactly the direction highlighted in the conclusion of `ocLTL` for safety-style / fixed-point-definable fragments. The mathematical research problem is to characterize when this symbolic iteration stays compact and how its complexity depends on the structure and specification.

## 4. Atomless Boolean algebra is the first laboratory

Asor's Boolean-algebra monograph develops decision procedures, quantifier elimination, recurrence relations, GSSOTC, and Tau over Boolean algebras, emphasizing the atomless case. Its minterm normal form gives an unusually explicit representation of finite tuple structure.

Source: Ohad Asor, *Theories and Applications of Boolean Algebras*, v0.29 (2024), TauNet.

### Derived baseline theorem: exact number of complete types

For the theory ABA of atomless Boolean algebras and k variables, there are `2^k` Boolean minterm cells. A tuple determines which of those cells are nonzero. Because ABA has quantifier elimination, this zero/nonzero support determines the complete type. Every nonempty support pattern is realizable by splitting 1 into finitely many nonzero pieces in an atomless Boolean algebra.

Therefore:

`|T_k(ABA)| = 2^(2^k) - 1.`

In particular:

- `|T_1| = 3`
- `|T_2| = 15`
- `|T_3| = 255`

A full proof is in `notes/ABA_TYPE_SPACE.md`; a finite exact enumerator is in `experiments/aba_types.py`.

This theorem is a baseline, not claimed novelty.

## 5. Adjacent 2026 synthesis frontier: a different finiteness mechanism

Dauvier, Filiot, and Reynier (CSL 2026) study register-bounded synthesis from Constraint LTL. Their generic finite reduction is based not on omega-categoricity but on **effective omega-regular satisfiability** of infinite constraint sequences; they also introduce **effective omega-regular approximability**, exact on lasso words, to cover domains such as `(N,<)` and `(Z,<)` where full omega-regular satisfiability fails.

Source: Nino Dauvier, Emmanuel Filiot, Pierre-Alain Reynier, *Register-Bounded Synthesis from Constraint LTL*, CSL 2026, LIPIcs 363:8, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.8.

This creates a real open comparison for OrbitSynthesis:

> How do effective omega-categoricity/type finiteness, completion, effective omega-regular satisfiability, and omega-regular approximability relate as sufficient mechanisms for finite reactive synthesis?

Do not assume implication in either direction without proof. `(N,<)` is already a warning: it is not omega-categorical, yet bounded synthesis is decidable by the CSL 2026 route.

## 6. Adjacent automata frontier: useful, but not yet our theorem

Balachander, Filiot, Gentilini, and Tzevelekos (MFCS 2025) develop permutation deterministic register automata, Myhill-Nerode-style congruences, canonical/minimal representations, polynomial-time equivalence, and polynomial-time minimization for fixed permutation policies.

Source: *Register Automata with Permutations*, MFCS 2025, LIPIcs.

Asor explicitly notes that when `G = Aut(M)`, orbit-finite state spaces coincide with the complete-type spaces `M^k / Aut(M)`, but also says the ocLTL equirealizability result seems to have no direct counterpart in the orbit-finite automata literature.

This makes a synthesis analogue of canonical/minimal residuals a legitimate **candidate research question**, but there is currently no basis in this repository for calling it the central frontier. It stays speculative until we derive a precise definition and either prove a nontrivial result or find a counterexample.

## 7. Ranked research directions

### Tier 1 — direct consequences of the frontier

**F1. Symbolic fixed-point synthesis over ABA.** Formalize the safety winning-region operator over the atomless Boolean algebra and implement formula-level iteration using BA quantifier elimination/equivalence checking. Compare against explicit type enumeration.

**F2. Specification-generated definable algebra.** Prove exact finite-algebra results for `B_Delta`; compute realized truth signatures and characterize when Delta attains the worst-case full type algebra.

**F3. Finiteness-mechanism comparison.** Compare omega-categorical type abstraction with effective omega-regular satisfiability / approximability. Seek implications, separations, and common generalizations.

### Tier 2 — likely valuable after Tier 1 is understood

**F4. Memory/decidability boundary.** Place bounded lookback, bounded registers, restricted recurrence, and known undecidability results on one precise map.

**F5. Complexity by orbit/type growth.** Relate practical synthesis blowup to orbit growth and to the structure of `B_Delta`, rather than only worst-case LTL complexity.

### Tier 3 — quarantined conjectural bridge

**F6. Myhill-Nerode / canonical synthesis quotients.** Investigate only after defining the relevant residual object for a two-player synthesis problem. Do not assume language-recognition minimization lifts to synthesis.

## 8. Research method

Use a two-certificate discipline:

1. **truth certificate:** proof, formal proof, exact exhaustive check, or a clearly scoped computational witness;
2. **understanding certificate:** identify the mechanism — symmetry, minterm support, quotient, invariant, extension property, or another structural reason.

Counterexample search precedes proof effort where possible. Representation changes are encouraged, but a representation change is not evidence. Every promoted conjecture must record assumptions, smallest tested cases, and an explicit falsification plan.
