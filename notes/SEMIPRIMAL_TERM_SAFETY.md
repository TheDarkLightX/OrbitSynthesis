# Exact original-signature term synthesis for semi-primal equation safety games

**Status:** DERIVED from the classical Foster–Pixley characterization of semi-primal term operations. The universal-algebra theorem is prior art; the finite safety-game specialization is under novelty review.

This note answers a question left open by `FINITE_BOOLEAN_POWER_SAFETY.md`:

> when can the finite positional lookup strategy be represented by terms of the **original finite algebra signature**, rather than by Boolean-skeleton patchwork / Tau representation bits?

For finite semi-primal algebras, the answer has an exact local form.

## 1. Semi-primality

Let L be a finite semi-primal algebra.

Classically:

> an operation `f:L^m -> L` is a term operation of L iff it preserves every subalgebra of L.

Equivalently, for every input tuple z,

`f(z) belongs to Sg_L(z)`,

where `Sg_L(z)` is the subalgebra generated by the coordinates of z.

### Proof of the equivalence with the pointwise condition

If f preserves subalgebras, apply it to the generated subalgebra `Sg(z)` containing all coordinates of z.

Conversely, if `f(z) in Sg(z)` for every z and all coordinates of z lie in a subalgebra S, then `Sg(z) subseteq S`, so `f(z) in S`. Hence f preserves S.

Semi-primality upgrades this preservation condition to term-definability.

## 2. Finite local safety game

Use the equation-only local game over L:

- state labels `a in L^k`;
- environment labels `u in L^p`;
- controller/next-state labels `v in L^k`;
- safe local relation `R subseteq L^k x L^p x L^k`, defined by the safety equations.

For an observation

`z=(a,u)`,

let

`G(z) := Sg_L({all coordinates of a and u})`.

Any k-output term controller must output inside

`G(z)^k`.

## 3. Term-admissible predecessor

For `W subseteq L^k`, define

`TPre_L(W)
 := { a in L^k : for every u in L^p,
      exists v in W intersect G(a,u)^k
      with (a,u,v) in R }.`

This is the ordinary safety predecessor with one additional local action filter: the chosen output values must lie in the subalgebra generated by the currently observed local values.

## 4. Exact term-controller theorem

### Theorem 1

Let L be finite semi-primal. Let

`W_* = nu W . TPre_L(W)`.

Then W_* is exactly the largest local state set on which the equation safety game has a **memoryless controller whose output coordinates are term operations of L**.

### Sufficiency

For every winning observation `(a,u)` choose one safe

`v(a,u) in W_* intersect G(a,u)^k`.

For observations whose state part is outside W_*, extend the table arbitrarily by choosing, say, one observed coordinate value for every output coordinate. Since k>=1, this always lies in the generated subalgebra.

The resulting total function

`sigma : L^(k+p) -> L^k`

has each coordinate satisfying

`sigma_j(z) in Sg_L(z)`

for every z. Therefore each coordinate preserves every subalgebra. Semi-primality makes each coordinate a term operation.

On W_* the chosen table is safe and remains in W_*, hence those terms form a winning positional controller.

### Necessity

Let a term controller be winning on some state set W.

Every term operation preserves subalgebras, so at every observation `(a,u)` its output lies in `G(a,u)^k`. Safety and invariance of W therefore imply

`W subseteq TPre_L(W)`.

Hence W is contained in the greatest fixed point W_*.

## 5. Primal case

A finite primal algebra has no proper nonempty subalgebras: otherwise an arbitrary function mapping an element of a proper subalgebra outside it could not be a term operation.

Thus for every nonempty observation tuple z,

`G(z)=L`.

The action filter disappears:

`TPre_L = Pre_L`.

This recovers `PRIMAL_ALGEBRA_SAFETY_LIFTING.md`: every finite positional strategy is term-definable.

## 6. A simple non-primal semi-primal shape

The 2024 semi-primal survey recalls that a prime field `Z/pZ` with only 0 named is semi-primal but not primal; `{0}` is a proper subalgebra.

For such an algebra:

- if every currently observed local value is 0, every original-signature term output must remain 0;
- once the observed values generate the whole field, the subalgebra filter becomes vacuous.

So term synthesis differs from arbitrary finite-table synthesis only on observations trapped in proper generated subalgebras.

This is exactly the kind of distinction the `TPre` operator captures.

## 7. Boolean-power consequence

Let A=L[B] be a Boolean power and consider equation-only safety.

`FINITE_BOOLEAN_POWER_SAFETY.md` says arbitrary causal realizability is governed by ordinary `Pre_L`.

If we additionally demand a controller given by **L-terms evaluated pointwise**, realizability from lifted states is governed by `TPre_L` instead.

Therefore there are two distinct synthesis semantics:

1. **patchwork strategy semantics** — any locally constant response in the Boolean power; finite operator `Pre_L`;
2. **original-signature term semantics** — response must be one global L-term table; finite operator `TPre_L` for semi-primal L.

Tau's binary representation layer can implement the first semantics even when the lookup table is not an L-term, because it exposes the Boolean skeleton explicitly.

## 8. Why this does NOT extend unchanged to inequation clauses

Equation-only safety needs one output label per constant joint `(state,input)` region. A term operation can provide exactly that.

Inequation clauses may require several distinct local output labels to be simultaneously nonzero inside one such region. The abstract atomless strategy achieves this by **splitting the Boolean region**.

A pointwise L-term evaluated on constant local inputs is constant on that whole region. It cannot create a new internal split.

### Minimal phenomenon

With no informative input distinction, a clause may require an output to realize two different local labels on nonzero subregions. Atomlessness makes such an output element exist, while no pointwise term of the constant observation can produce it.

Therefore:

- `TPre` is an exact term-synthesis theorem for equation-only games;
- clause/hypergraph synthesis needs an effective Boolean-skeleton split/witness mechanism and should not be advertised as ordinary term synthesis without extra resources.

## 9. Quasi-primal frontier

A finite quasi-primal algebra has a larger term clone constraint: term operations are characterized by preservation of internal isomorphisms between subalgebras.

Semi-primality is the special case where the only internal isomorphisms are identities on subalgebras.

This suggests the next hierarchy:

- **primal:** no local restriction;
- **semi-primal:** local generated-subalgebra restriction `v in G(a,u)^k`;
- **quasi-primal:** generated-subalgebra restriction plus equivariance/coupling across internal-isomorphism-related observations;
- **general finite algebra:** strategy table must belong to the full term clone.

Do not assume the quasi-primal case reduces to the same pointwise TPre: nontrivial internal isomorphisms can couple choices at distinct observations. Construct a counterexample or prove an orbit-representative selection theorem before promoting a formula.

## 10. Algorithmic value

For a fixed small semi-primal L:

1. precompute the generated subalgebra `G(z)` for every local observation z;
2. delete controller actions outside `G(z)^k`;
3. solve the resulting finite safety game normally;
4. record the finite winning strategy table;
5. interpolate each coordinate to an L-term using the semi-primal term-function machinery.

The infinite Boolean-power term-synthesis problem is thereby reduced to a finite safety game plus finite universal-algebraic interpolation.

## 11. Prior-art discipline

The characterization of semi-primal term functions as subalgebra-preserving operations is classical Foster–Pixley theory and explicitly restated in modern semi-primal work.

Potential synthesis contribution:

> use that preservation theorem as an exact action filter in causal safety games, thereby separating arbitrary Boolean-power patchwork realizability from original-signature term realizability.
