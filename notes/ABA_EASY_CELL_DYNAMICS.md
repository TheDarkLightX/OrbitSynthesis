# Easy and algebraic classes of ABA cell obligation dynamics

**Status:** DERIVED structural criteria for the cell predecessor `barL`; implementation heuristics pending. These are sufficient conditions, not an exhaustive tractability classification.

`ABA_MONOTONE_DYNAMICS_UNIVERSALITY.md` shows that unrestricted cell dynamics can realize arbitrary bottom/top-preserving monotone Boolean networks. Therefore useful algorithms need to recognize **structured subclasses**.

Let

`L : P(A*) -> P(A*)`

be the zero-safe controllable-predecessor map on state-cell targets.

Always:

`L(empty)=empty`,

`L(A*)=A*`,

and L is monotone.

## 1. System-stutter condition -> extensive dynamics

For every zero-safe state cell v and every environment refinement p over v, suppose the system has an allowed response whose next cell is again v.

Formally:

`for every v in A*, for every p over v,
 exists allowed u over p with next(u)=v.`

### Theorem 1

Under this condition,

`G subseteq L(G)`

for every `G subseteq A*`.

### Proof

If `v in G`, then under every environment refinement p the assumed stuttering response leads to `v in G`. Hence `v in L({v})`. By monotonicity,

`v in L({v}) subseteq L(G)`.

So every member of G is retained.

### Consequences

Each individual orbit is increasing:

`G subseteq L(G) subseteq L^2(G) ...`

and stabilizes after at most `|A*|-|G|` strict additions.

More strongly, for OrbitSynthesis's **antichain closure**, every later orbit mask is a superset of the seed and therefore redundant. Thus a positive seed family B generates **no new irredundant obligations at all**:

`H* = Min(B)`

unless B is already inconsistent.

So universal system-stutter is an especially strong fast-path criterion.

## 2. Environment-pin condition -> contractive dynamics

For every zero-safe state cell v, suppose there exists an environment refinement p over v for which the only zero-safe allowed next cell is v itself.

Formally:

`for every v in A*, exists p over v such that
  allowed_safe_next(p) = {v}.`

### Theorem 2

Under this condition,

`L(G) subseteq G`

for every G.

### Proof

Fix `v notin G`. Choose the pinning environment refinement p. Its only safe next cell is v, which is not in G, so there is no allowed response into G. Hence `v notin L(G)`.

Therefore no cell outside G can enter L(G).

### Consequences

Each orbit is decreasing and reaches a fixed point after at most |G| strict removals.

For antichain orbit closure, only the **eventual smallest mask** matters: every earlier orbit mask is a superset and becomes redundant.

If the orbit reaches empty, the winning region is False.

## 3. Both conditions -> identity dynamics

If the system can always stutter and the environment can force stutter at every cell, then L is both extensive and contractive.

Therefore

`L(G)=G`

for all G.

The positive fixed point is just the normalized seed family with no propagation.

## 4. These conditions are exact one-point tests

The extensivity criterion can be checked from singleton targets:

`L is extensive`

iff

`v in L({v})` for every v.

The contractivity criterion can be checked from co-singleton targets:

`L is contractive`

iff

`v notin L(A*\{v})` for every v.

The cell-game definitions turn these directly into the stutter/pin transition conditions above.

Thus no symbolic powerset exploration is required to detect either class.

## 5. Deterministic dynamics

Suppose every zero-safe partial cell p has exactly one allowed zero-safe next cell

`delta(p) in A*`.

Then

`v in L(G)`

iff every environment refinement p over v has `delta(p) in G`.

### No essential environment branching

If delta depends only on the current state cell v (all p over v choose the same next cell), write

`pi(v)=delta(p)`.

Then

`L(G)=pi^{-1}(G)`.

This is ordinary inverse image under a finite function.

If pi is a permutation, L is a Boolean-lattice automorphism.

## 6. Permutation dynamics should be solved algebraically

Let pi be a permutation of A* and seed obligation H.

Then

`L^t(H)=pi^{-t}(H)`.

The logical orbit is the orbit of H under the cyclic group generated by pi.

Instead of applying L until a set repeats:

1. compute the cycle decomposition of pi;
2. determine the period of H's membership pattern on each cycle;
3. take the least common multiple of those local periods;
4. retain an **orbit descriptor** `(pi,H,period)` when downstream code can consume one.

This computes the orbit period in time polynomial in `|A*|` and integer-arithmetic costs, even when the period itself is exponentially larger than a compact source description.

Materializing every translated H may still require output proportional to the orbit length; the algebraic fast path separates **solving** from **expanding the answer**.

## 7. Exact subset period under a permutation

Let pi decompose into point cycles of lengths

`ell_1,...,ell_m`.

For one point cycle C of length ell, write H's membership on C as a cyclic binary word of length ell.

Let

`p_C`

be the smallest positive rotation period of that word.

Then the subset H returns to itself under pi^t exactly when t is a multiple of every p_C.

Therefore:

### Theorem 3

`period_pi(H) = lcm_C p_C`.

For a singleton contained in a point cycle of length ell,

`p_C=ell`.

For the LFSR construction in `ABA_LFSR_LOWER_BOUND.md`, a nonzero singleton therefore has period `2^d-1`.

## 8. Fixed-point obligations under a permutation

The final positive family generated by seed set B is the inclusion-minimal family among all cyclic translates

`{pi^{-t}(H): H in B, t>=0}`.

Since pi preserves cardinality and set inclusion,

- all translates of one seed have the same cardinality;
- two distinct translates of the same seed are therefore incomparable;
- redundancy can only arise between orbits of seeds of different sizes or when two orbits intersect.

This makes antichain minimization substantially easier than for a general monotone map.

## 9. Singleton seeds have a closed form

If H is a singleton `{v}`, its orbit is the set of singletons corresponding to the point cycle Orb_pi(v).

Requiring the complete support S to hit every orbit singleton is equivalent to

`Orb_pi(v) subseteq S`.

Thus the entire exponentially/long iterated clause family can be summarized by one **required-cell mask** at a richer representation level.

For multiple singleton seeds, take the union of their point orbits.

This exactly compresses the LFSR lower-bound family's positive result:

`required cells = all nonzero LFSR states`.

## 10. General deterministic non-bijective maps

For a finite function pi that is not injective,

`L(G)=pi^{-1}(G)`

still holds.

Functional-graph decomposition gives directed cycles with in-trees. Inverse-image iteration can be analyzed by graph algorithms rather than generic Boolean-network iteration.

A seed's backward orbit expands through predecessor trees and eventually becomes periodic according to the terminal cycles. Depending on the seed, L may be extensive on some regions and not others.

This deserves its own exact algorithm if deterministic Tau steps are common.

## 11. Why the representation should be polymorphic

The same semantic obligation family may be best stored as:

- explicit antichain masks for small/general instances;
- a required-cell mask for singleton permutation orbits;
- permutation-orbit descriptors;
- a BDD when crossing width is small;
- a clause hypergraph when OBDDs are bad;
- another algebraic object for linear dynamics.

All share the same cell-game semantics and can be converted/falsified against the explicit mask oracle.
