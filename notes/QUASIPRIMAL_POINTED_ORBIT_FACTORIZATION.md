# Pointed generated-subalgebra factorization for quasi-primal term synthesis

**Status:** DERIVED from the classical quasi-primal preservation characterization; bounded oracle complete on two three-element calibrations; Morph minimum-sufficient-abstraction certificate complete on the bounded Quackenbush corpus; novelty review incomplete; not Lean-checked.

## Result

For a finite quasi-primal algebra `Q` and arity `n>1`, an `n`-ary term operation is exactly:

> one independently chosen seed in each isomorphism class of pointed generated subalgebras, transported uniquely through that class.

This removes the redundant raw table variables and internal-isomorphism equalities from the static quasi-primal synthesis layer. It also yields a direct linear-time algorithm, for fixed `Q` and explicit sparse input, for **list term interpolation**.

The reactive domain-selection problem remains. The known no-greatest-region witness becomes one empty transported-list intersection inside a single pointed class.

## Classical input

Let `Q` be finite quasi-primal. An internal isomorphism is an isomorphism `alpha:S->T` between subalgebras of `Q`.

Pixley's classical characterization says that, for `n>1`, a total operation `f:Q^n->Q` is a `Q`-term exactly when it preserves every internal isomorphism:

- for `z in S^n`, `f(z) in S`;
- `f(alpha z)=alpha(f(z))`.

The first item follows by using the identity isomorphism on `S`.

## Pointed classes and unique transport

For `z=(z_1,...,z_n)`, let `Sg(z)` be the subalgebra generated by its coordinates. Define

`z ~ z'`

iff there is an isomorphism

`alpha:Sg(z)->Sg(z')`

with `alpha(z_i)=z'_i` for every `i`.

Let `P_n(Q)=Q^n/~`. Choose one representative `r_C` for each class `C`, and put `S_C=Sg(r_C)`.

### Lemma 1 — unique pointed transport

For every `z in C`, there is a unique isomorphism

`theta_z:S_C->Sg(z)`

with `theta_z(r_C)=z` coordinatewise.

Existence is the definition of the class. Uniqueness holds because the coordinates of `r_C` generate `S_C`; two homomorphisms agreeing on them agree everywhere. This removes every path/gauge choice.

## Exact product theorem

Let `Term_n(Q)` be the set of `n`-ary original-signature term operations.

### Theorem 2 — pointed product factorization

Evaluation on representatives gives a bijection

`Term_n(Q) ~= product_(C in P_n(Q)) S_C`.

The forward map is

`f |-> (f(r_C))_C`.

Given arbitrary seeds `a_C in S_C`, the inverse is

`f_a(z)=theta_z(a_C)` for `z in C`.

### Proof

If `f` is a term, identity preservation gives `f(r_C) in S_C`, and preservation of `theta_z` gives

`f(z)=theta_z(f(r_C))`.

Thus representative seeds determine the full table.

Conversely choose arbitrary seeds and define `f_a` by transport. Let `psi:S->T` be an internal isomorphism and `z in S^n`. The restrictions

`theta_(psi z)` and `(psi|Sg(z)) o theta_z`

both send `r_C` to `psi z`; Lemma 1 makes them equal. Hence

`f_a(psi z)=psi(f_a(z))`,

and `f_a(z) in Sg(z) subseteq S`. Pixley's characterization makes `f_a` a term. QED.

Consequently

`|Term_n(Q)| = product_C |S_C|`.

### Minimum-sufficiency consequence

There are no false merges: one seed has one unambiguous transport law throughout its class. There are no false splits: arbitrary seeds in distinct classes extend simultaneously, so distinct classes carry independent residual choices.

This is a structural Cartesian-product minimum, not a claim about the fewest bits under every coding convention.

## Sparse list interpolation

Let `T subseteq Q^n` be sparse, and give every `z in T` an allowed list `L_z subseteq Q`. For each pointed class define

`A_C = intersection_(z in T intersect C) theta_z^(-1)(L_z intersect Sg(z))`.

For unconstrained classes take `A_C=S_C`.

### Theorem 3 — exact list criterion

The list instance is term-interpolable iff every active `A_C` is nonempty.

Necessity: one term supplies one seed per class, and every list pulls back to a restriction on that seed. Sufficiency: choose one seed in each nonempty intersection and apply Theorem 2.

### Fixed-`Q` complexity

Precompute the finite subalgebra/internal-isomorphism catalog. For each explicit tuple, compute `Sg(z)`, canonicalize it through the constant-size catalog, pull its list back, and intersect a constant-size mask. The deterministic running time is

`O(total encoded tuple length + total explicit list length)`,

with a constant depending on `Q`.

This resolves the fixed-quasi-primal side of the list-interpolation target in `TERM_SYNTHESIS_COMPLEXITY_BOUNDARIES.md`.

The output is a compact orbit-seed representation. Compiling it to a short original-signature term/circuit is a separate problem.

## Vector controllers and fixed-domain safety

A `k`-coordinate controller uses one vector seed `a_C in S_C^k`. At observation `z in C`,

`sigma(z)=theta_z^k(a_C)`.

For a fixed candidate state domain `W`, let `Safe_W(z) subseteq Q^k` be the safe outputs whose next state lies in `W`. Define

`A_C(W) = intersection_(active z in C)
          theta_z^(-k)(Safe_W(z) intersect Sg(z)^k)`.

### Corollary 4

One original-signature term controller keeps `W` safe iff every active `A_C(W)` is nonempty. Choose one vector seed per class and transport it.

Thus fixed-domain quasi-primal term safety is not a generic globally entangled table CSP. It factorizes into independent finite list intersections once `W` is fixed. Searching or optimizing `W` remains global.

## Exact specialization of subpower row-list synthesis

`SUBPOWER_ROW_LIST_SYNTHESIS.md` represents scalar term evaluations on an active observation set `Z` by a generated subpower `P_Z subseteq Q^Z`. Theorem 2 identifies it explicitly:

`P_Z ~= product_(pointed classes C meeting Z) S_C`,

embedded by the transports `theta_z`.

Therefore

`tau(P_Z^k) intersection Product_(z in Z) L_z`

splits exactly into the class intersections above. The earlier phrase “subject to cycle consistency” is unnecessary here: an internal-isomorphism loop fixing the generating tuple fixes its generated subalgebra pointwise.

The raw-table formulation in `QUASIPRIMAL_TERM_SAFETY_CSP.md` remains correct, but it is overparameterized: one class seed replaces every raw variable/equality component, and termhood holds by construction.

## Existing no-greatest witness

In `QUASIPRIMAL_NO_GREATEST_REGION.md`, observations `000` and `111` share one pointed class under the internal swap `phi:0<->1` on `{0,1}`.

For `W={00,01,11}`, both locally allow only `01`. At representative `000`, the pulled-back list is `{01}`. Pulling the list at `111` back through `phi` gives `{10}`. Hence

`{01} intersection {10} = empty`.

Each separate winning domain activates only one side, so its class intersection is nonempty. The no-greatest theorem survives; the quotient exposes its inner mechanism.

## Demi-semi-primal simplification

If `D` is demi-semi-primal, every internal isomorphism extends to a global automorphism. Pointed classes then coincide with `Aut(D)` tuple orbits.

Moreover, if an automorphism fixes an observation tuple `z`, it fixes every term value on `z`, hence fixes `Sg(z)` pointwise. Therefore

`v in Sg(z)^k  =>  v is fixed by the stabilizer of z`.

The explicit stabilizer-fixed filter in `DEMI_SEMIPRIMAL_TERM_SAFETY.md` is redundant. Its predecessor can use only

`safe intersection target intersection Sg(z)^k`.

The set iteration is the same local generated-subalgebra predecessor as in the semi-primal case. The difference is reconstruction: semi-primal choices are local, while demi-semi-primal choices are selected once per global orbit and transported.

## Prior-art and scope boundary

Classical ingredients include Pixley's internal-isomorphism characterization, Quackenbusch's demi-semi-primal extension hierarchy, Baker–Pixley interpolation, and the modern SMP/partial-interpolation literature.

The product theorem is a short structural consequence of classical preservation theory and may already exist under older terminology. Do not claim it as a new universal-algebra theorem without closer old-source review.

Under review are the synthesis-specific uses: the Morph minimum-abstraction certificate, quotient compilation of fixed-domain safety, the one-intersection explanation of the no-greatest witness, and removal of the redundant stabilizer filter.

Limits:

- the invoked Pixley statement is used only for `n>1`; unary operations can be precomputed separately for fixed finite `Q`;
- `Q` is fixed in the linear-time claim;
- succinct list encodings require their own evaluation cost;
- semantic seed construction does not bound explicit term size;
- fixed-domain factorization does not make domain selection union-closed;
- finite checks are not the generic proof;
- no publication novelty or patent freedom-to-operate conclusion is made.
