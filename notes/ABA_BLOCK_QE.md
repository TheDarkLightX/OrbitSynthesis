# Direct block quantifier elimination for atomless Boolean algebra

**Status:** DERIVED semantic theorem; exhaustive randomized finite-support checks completed for small `(k,r)`; exact novelty NOT claimed. Marriott–Odersky (1996) already emphasize independence of inequations, and Asor's GS Corollary 19 is the one-bound-variable case. The direct all-at-once block formulation and its role as a support-symbolic compiler fast path are under novelty review.

## 1. Problem shape

Let `X=(x_1,...,x_k)` be free Boolean-algebra variables and let

`Y=(y_1,...,y_r)`

be an existential block. Let `f(X,Y)` and `g_i(X,Y)` be Boolean-algebra terms.

Consider the normalized clause

`exists Y . [ f(X,Y)=0 AND AND_i g_i(X,Y)!=0 ].`

Asor's one-variable ABA quantifier-elimination theorem treats `r=1`. The support representation exposes a direct block rule for arbitrary finite `r`.

## 2. Cofactor notation

For every bit vector

`b in {0,1}^r`,

write

`f_b(X) := f(X,b)`

and

`g_(i,b)(X) := g_i(X,b)`,

where the bound BA variables are syntactically replaced by `0` or `1` according to `b`.

Define two families of terms on `X` only:

`Block_f(X) := AND_{b in {0,1}^r} f_b(X)`

and

`Witness_(f,g_i)(X)
 := OR_{b in {0,1}^r} [ NOT f_b(X) AND g_(i,b)(X) ].`

All `AND`, `OR`, and `NOT` here are Boolean-algebra meet, join, and complement.

## 3. The block elimination theorem

### Theorem

In every atomless Boolean algebra,

`exists Y . [ f(X,Y)=0 AND AND_i g_i(X,Y)!=0 ]`

is equivalent to

`Block_f(X)=0
 AND
 AND_i Witness_(f,g_i)(X)!=0.`

Equivalently,

`exists Y . [ f=0 AND AND_i g_i!=0 ]`

iff

`[ AND_b f_b = 0 ]
 AND
 AND_i [ OR_b (NOT f_b AND g_(i,b)) != 0 ].`

For `r=1`, this becomes

`f(0) f(1)=0`

and

`[f(0)' g_i(0)] OR [f(1)' g_i(1)] != 0`,

which is Asor's Corollary 19 up to ordering of the two summands.

## 4. Support proof: the inner ground

Fix a value of the free tuple `X`. Let the nonzero atoms of the finite Boolean subalgebra generated by `X` and any interpreted coefficients be the **coarse regions**.

Inside one coarse region `v`, the r bound variables split it into

`2^r`

fine Venn cells indexed by `b in {0,1}^r`.

Because the ambient Boolean algebra is atomless, **every nonempty subset of those `2^r` fine cells is realizable**, independently in every coarse region.

Now inspect the equation `f=0`.

For a coarse region `v`, a fine cell `(v,b)` is forbidden exactly when

`f_b=1`

on that region.

Therefore a coarse region can be extended at all iff not every fine cell is forbidden. The region is impossible exactly when

`AND_b f_b = 1`.

Globally, every nonzero coarse region must be extendable, which is exactly

`Block_f=0`.

Assume this equation condition holds. Instead of searching for a delicate witness, choose the **maximal allowed refinement** in every nonzero coarse region: make *every* fine cell with `f_b=0` nonzero.

This one choice simultaneously satisfies all inequations `g_i!=0` exactly when, for every `i`, there is some nonzero coarse region and some fine label `b` such that

`f_b=0` and `g_(i,b)=1`.

That is precisely

`Witness_(f,g_i)!=0`.

No competition between inequations remains: if two inequations need different fine cells inside the same coarse region, atomlessness lets both cells be nonzero simultaneously.

This is the structural reason behind the "independence of inequations" phenomenon.

## 5. Support-bit form

Let the coarse Venn cells of `X` be indexed by

`v in U_k={0,1}^k`,

and let `z_v` mean that coarse cell `v` is nonzero.

Let `F subseteq U_k x {0,1}^r` be the minterm support of `f`, and `G_i` that of `g_i`.

Define

`Bad_F := { v : for every b, (v,b) in F }`

and

`H_i := { v : exists b . (v,b) notin F AND (v,b) in G_i }`.

Then the entire existential block projects to the propositional CNF

`AND_{v in Bad_F} NOT z_v
 AND
 AND_i OR_{v in H_i} z_v.`

So one normalized ABA clause projects to:

- negative unit clauses ruling out coarse regions with no allowed refinement;
- one positive support clause per inequation.

No quantified support bits need remain.

## 6. Closure of clause shape

The support formula translates back into the same ABA constraint shape.

Let

`B_F(X)`

be the Boolean term whose truth table is `Bad_F`, i.e. `B_F = Block_f`.

For each `i`, let

`H_i(X)=Witness_(f,g_i)`.

Then the projected formula is again

`B_F(X)=0 AND AND_i H_i(X)!=0`.

Thus a whole existential block can be removed **without sequentially eliminating its variables** and without changing the equation-plus-inequations clause schema.

## 7. Complexity of the direct support projection

Suppose the term truth tables over the `k+r` variables are available.

There are

`2^k * 2^r = 2^(k+r)`

fine cells.

Computing `Bad_F` requires one scan of the fine cells. Each `H_i` requires one additional scan (or a bit-parallel mask operation).

Hence the projection is linear in the explicit minterm truth-table size times the number of inequations:

`O((1+m) 2^(k+r))`

for `m` inequations, with word-parallel implementations reducing constants substantially.

This does **not** claim polynomial complexity in the compact syntax size of arbitrary Boolean terms; truth-table expansion can itself be exponential in arity.

## 8. Why this matters for Tau's current pipeline

At Tau commit `fd137e860b60083b36f9159ec8090cb1a3c3cb5a`, quantifier normalization groups same-kind/same-BA-type variables into blocks, then `anti_prenex_block` uses several fast paths and otherwise performs Boole/Shannon decomposition on whole atomic formulas. The implementation has a shared per-block split budget before unresolved residue is rewrapped/fallback processing is attempted.

For a homogeneous atomless-BA block whose dependent matrix is already one equation-plus-inequations clause, the theorem above suggests a more direct fast path:

1. compile the relevant terms to minterm/support masks once;
2. project the **entire existential block simultaneously** with `Bad_F` and `H_i` masks;
3. rebuild the quantifier-free clause;
4. optionally differential-check against the existing Tau path during development.

This is not "use BDDs instead of Tau." Tau already contains Boole/BDD-style decomposition at the formula-atom level. The proposed layer is theory-specific and lower-level: it works on ABA minterm-support coordinates and exploits atomless extension geometry before generic formula-atom splitting.

## 9. Product/packed-variable relevance

The theorem is already a block theorem, so it fits the ocLTL product reduction directly.

If one packed data value represents `r` underlying ABA coordinates, eliminating that packed value is exactly the case of eliminating an r-variable BA block at once. There is no need to sequentially expose the packed coordinates merely for the ABA existential projection.

## 10. Interpreted constants

The proof also works in the presence of finitely many interpreted constants.

Refine the coarse regions by the nonzero atoms of the finite subalgebra generated by those constants, as in `ABA_INTERPRETED_CONSTANTS.md`. On each resulting region, every Boolean term cofactor is either 0 or the whole region, and atomlessness again realizes every nonempty fine support.

The same formulas

`Block_f = AND_b f_b`

and

`Witness_(f,g_i)=OR_b (NOT f_b AND g_(i,b))`

remain correct.

The semantic support dimension scales with the constant-partition rank `rho(C)`.

## 11. Relation to prior work

- **Boole variable elimination** already removes variables from systems of Boolean equations.
- **Marriott–Odersky, Negative Boolean Constraints (TCS 1996)** develops variable elimination for equations plus inequations over atomless Boolean algebras and explicitly identifies independence of inequations as the mechanism lifting single-inequation results to systems.
- **Asor, Guarded Successor (2024), Corollary 19** gives the one-variable formula corresponding to `r=1` above and uses it in Tau's modern QE development.

Therefore the logical eliminability and inequation-independence phenomenon are not claimed novel. The questions still open for OrbitSynthesis are:

1. whether this explicit simultaneous-block support formulation appears verbatim in prior work;
2. whether compiling whole Tau quantifier blocks through it avoids measurable intermediate blowup;
3. whether the same support representation can be retained into ocLTL feasibility and fixed-point synthesis, yielding an end-to-end algorithmic improvement rather than only a different QE proof.

## 12. Falsification and validation

A bounded exhaustive checker should compare, for random fine-cell masks `F,G_i`:

- exhaustive enumeration of every allowed fine support extending a coarse support; versus
- the projected condition `Bad_F/H_i`.

Checks already performed privately for random instances with `(k,r)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)` found no divergence. Commit a reproducible checker next.

The universal theorem rests on the structural proof above, not on those finite checks.
