# Positive-signature dominance: a fragment-relative criterion for extremal synthesis

**Status:** DERIVED finite-order theorem with standard homogeneous-structure examples. The order-theoretic lemma is elementary; the research direction is its use as an automatic synthesis-fragment classifier over finite/omega-categorical type fibers.

The ABA greatest-response theorem suggests a broader question:

> When can the system replace existential response search by one response that is at least as good as every other response for all predicates used positively by the specification?

The right notion is not necessarily a semilattice operation supplied by the ambient structure. It can be read directly from **positive type signatures**.

## 1. Positive observation signature

Let E be a finite response fiber: the complete response types compatible with the current partial/environment type.

Let

`Delta={delta_1,...,delta_m}`

be a finite family of response predicates that the target fragment uses only positively.

For `e in E`, define its positive signature

`sigma(e) = { j in [m] : delta_j(e) is true } subseteq [m].`

Define the fragment preorder

`e <=_Delta e' iff sigma(e) subseteq sigma(e')`.

Every monotone Boolean formula in the truth values of Delta is upward in this preorder.

## 2. Exact dominance criterion

Let

`U_Delta(E) = UNION_{e in E} sigma(e)`

be the union of all realized positive signatures.

### Theorem 1 — union-realizability iff a greatest positive response exists

The following are equivalent:

1. `(E, <=_Delta)` has a greatest element;
2. there exists `e_max in E` such that

   `sigma(e_max)=U_Delta(E)`;

3. the union of all realized positive signatures is itself realized.

### Proof

If `e_max` is greatest, every realized signature is contained in `sigma(e_max)`, so their union is contained in `sigma(e_max)`. The reverse inclusion is immediate because `e_max` participates in the union.

If the union signature is realized by `e_max`, then every realized signature is contained in it, so `e_max` is greatest.

## 3. Dominance for every positive Boolean objective

Let `F:{0,1}^m -> {0,1}` be monotone coordinatewise.

### Theorem 2 — one witness dominates all positive formulas

If `e_max` realizes the union signature, then

`exists e in E. F(sigma(e))=1`

iff

`F(sigma(e_max))=1`.

### Proof

The reverse direction is trivial. For the forward direction, if e satisfies F then

`sigma(e) subseteq sigma(e_max)`.

Monotonicity of F gives the result.

Thus existential search over E collapses to one response for the entire positive Boolean algebra generated by Delta.

## 4. Automatic classifier

For an effectively finite type fiber, dominance can be detected without guessing a semilattice operation:

1. compute the Delta-signature of every response type, or symbolically compute their union;
2. form U, the union signature;
3. ask whether U is realizable by some response type in the fiber.

If yes, choose any realizing response as the fragment-dominant response.

In an effectively omega-categorical structure this becomes a finite type/extension question. In other decidable structures it may be implemented directly as satisfiability of the fiber constraints plus all predicates in U.

## 5. ABA as the strongest case

For ABA's support-positive fragment, take Delta to be the fine-cell nonzeroness predicates

`C_v != 0`.

A response type's signature is exactly its support.

Under fixed zero/equation constraints and a fixed partial support P, the union of all zero-feasible response supports is precisely

`Max(P) = every zero-allowed fine cell above P`.

Atomlessness realizes this union support whenever the fiber is nonempty.

Hence ABA satisfies union-realizability for the full fine-support positive fragment, not merely for a small selected Delta.

This recovers `ABA_EXTREMAL_RESPONSE_PRINCIPLE.md`.

## 6. Countable random (Rado) graph

Fix distinct parameters `a_1,...,a_k` and restrict to a **fresh** response vertex y.

Take

`Delta_adj = { E(y,a_i) : i=1,...,k }`.

By the Rado extension property, every subset A of the parameters is realized as the neighborhood pattern of some fresh y.

Therefore the realized signatures are all of `P([k])`, their union is `[k]`, and a fresh vertex adjacent to every parameter realizes it.

### Corollary

For objectives positive in adjacency-to-current-parameter predicates, the fresh-vertex response fiber has a dominant response type: "adjacent to all parameters."

### Polarity warning

If both `E(y,a_i)` and `not E(y,a_i)` are included as independent **positive** atoms, the union signature contains both opposite literals and is not realizable. Dominance disappears.

So the property belongs to `(structure, fragment, polarity)`, not the structure alone.

## 7. Henson K_n-free graph

Let `H_n` be the countable universal homogeneous K_n-free graph.

Fix a finite parameter-induced graph G and a fresh response vertex y.

A candidate positive adjacency signature is a subset

`A subseteq V(G)`

such that y is adjacent exactly to A.

By the Henson extension property, A is realizable iff A contains no `(n-1)`-clique of G; otherwise y together with that clique would form K_n.

Thus the realized positive signatures are exactly the K_(n-1)-clique-free subsets of V(G).

Their union is all of V(G), because every singleton is allowed.

### Theorem 3 — local Henson dominance criterion

The positive-adjacency response fiber has a greatest response iff G contains no K_(n-1).

Equivalently, dominance holds exactly on those parameter types for which adjacency to **every** current parameter remains K_n-free.

This is a useful intermediate regime: the theory does not have global dominance, but the finite type graph can tell us locally whether the current state lies in a dominant-response region.

## 8. Pure equality

Let the parameter tuple contain b distinct equality classes.

### Equality-positive fragment

Take

`Delta_eq = { y=a_i }`

one representative per distinct class.

Different equality signatures are mutually exclusive. If `b>=2`, the union signature contains at least two equalities and is unrealizable.

So there is no dominant response for equality-positive objectives.

### Disequality-positive fragment

Take

`Delta_neq = { y!=a_i }`.

A fresh element realizes every disequality simultaneously in an infinite equality structure. Therefore the union signature is realized and the fresh type is dominant.

Again polarity changes the answer inside the same theory.

## 9. Dense linear order without endpoints

Let distinct parameters be ordered

`a_1 < ... < a_k`.

### Right-positive fragment

For

`Delta_R = { a_i < y }`,

a point y to the right of all parameters realizes the union signature, hence is dominant.

### Left-positive fragment

Similarly `Delta_L={y<a_i}` has a dominant response below all parameters.

### Mixed orientation

If both enough left- and right-facing inequalities are declared positive, the union signature can require simultaneously

`a_k < y`

and

`y < a_1`,

which is impossible. Dominance fails.

## 10. Relation to semilattice closure

A genuine join-semilattice operation on response codes that preserves realizability is a sufficient mechanism for union-realizability, but it is not logically necessary.

The signature theorem is more primitive:

> dominance only needs the coordinatewise union of **all realized positive signatures** to be realized.

This may hold even when no useful global polymorphism/join operation is available.

Conversely, semilattice polymorphisms in relational structures are a natural structural sufficient condition for closure of positive primitive constraints, so polymorphism theory remains a promising source of broad tractable families.

Do not claim that a semilattice polymorphism is necessary or that it characterizes omega-categorical tractability.

## 11. Dynamic synthesis version

For a reactive game, the criterion can be applied fiber-by-fiber.

At every partial/environment type p:

1. compute its legal response type fiber `E_p`;
2. compute the positive observation signature family relevant to the selected objective abstraction;
3. test union-realizability;
4. if successful, replace system existential search at p by its dominant response.

This yields a **partially collapsed game**:

- dominant fibers become deterministic system responses;
- non-dominant fibers retain explicit system branching.

ABA's protected positive fragment is the extreme case where every legal fiber collapses.

Henson graphs provide a natural mixed example where dominance depends on the current finite type.

## 12. A quantitative parameter

Define the **dominance deficit** of a finite signature family S by

`def(S) = min_{s in S} |(UNION S) \ s|`.

Then:

- deficit 0 iff a dominant response exists;
- small deficit means some response misses only a few independently desirable positive observations.

This parameter may support bounded-branching synthesis algorithms: branch only on the missing positive coordinates rather than on all complete response types.

**Status:** the deficit algorithmic use is a conjectural next direction; the definition itself is elementary.

## 13. Research program

1. Prove exact fragment-relative dominance criteria for standard homogeneous structures.
2. Relate union-realizability to polymorphism clones / pp-definability where possible.
3. Develop partially collapsed game algorithms when only some fibers are dominant.
4. Study dominance deficit and the number of maximal positive signatures when no top exists.
5. Determine whether bounded maximal-signature width is preserved by reactive fixed-point operators.
6. Integrate the classifier into OrbitSynthesis backend selection.

The central conceptual shift is:

> ask whether the **positive signatures actually observed by the specification** have a greatest realized union, rather than asking whether the ambient theory globally has a preferred join operation.
